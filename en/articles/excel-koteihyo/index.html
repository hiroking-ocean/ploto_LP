You Don't Have to Build Excel Gantt Charts and WBS in Excel | Create Them in a Free App and Export to Excel

Building a project schedule or WBS in Excel means drawing borders, typing dates, and coloring cells by hand. With the free Windows app Ploto, you just enter tasks, link them, and export straight to Excel.

Published: November 2, 2026

You Sat Down to Plan a Schedule, Not Color Excel Cells

“Please send the schedule in Excel.” “Submit the WBS in Excel too.” One request from a client, and there goes your afternoon—lining up dates, drawing borders, numbering rows, and coloring cells one by one. You haven’t even started thinking about the actual project plan, and it’s already evening. The finished sheet barely fits on a small screen, leaving you leaning in and squinting to trace tiny text row by row.

Learning spreadsheet formulas might make it a little easier. But what you actually need isn’t Excel expertise—it’s a schedule you can hand over.

With the project scheduling app Ploto, you don’t need to memorize formulas or draw borders. Enter a task and a clean bar appears; link tasks with an arrow and dates adjust automatically. When a schedule gets dense and hard to read, you can increase row height or hold Ctrl + scroll to zoom the timeline.

Export your finished schedule as an .xlsx file. Both the Gantt chart and Excel export are free with no time limit. Even when a client asks for a schedule or WBS in Excel, you can hand it over as is.

From Creating a Schedule to Exporting to Excel

  1. Install Ploto
    Get it for free from the Microsoft Store on Windows.
  2. Enter your tasks
    Add tasks with the + button. Drag the bar that appears to set its dates.
    You can also start from an existing Excel schedule (see below).
  3. Link tasks with arrows
    Drag the round dot at the end of a bar to the next task. That sets the dependency between them.
  4. Export to Excel
    Open “Export WBS (Excel)” from the “File” menu, check the preview, and press “Export”.

In Excel, a schedule change means rewriting dates down the sheet and recoloring cells. In Ploto, you just move one bar and export again.

Ready for WBS Submissions in Excel Too

Organize tasks into parent-child hierarchies and the exported Excel file automatically gets WBS numbers like 1.1 and 1.2. There is no limit on hierarchy depth.

In the export dialog, you can adjust the following while checking the live preview on the right. All of these settings are free too:

  • Layout: “Roadmap” for a high-level overview, “Detailed WBS” showing hierarchy and assignees, or “Formal WBS” with emphasized grid borders
  • Columns: Choose which columns to include, and reorder or rename headers
  • Colors: Bar color scheme, header colors, font, and text size
  • Holidays: Color-coding for Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays
  • Scope: Select only the tasks you want to submit

Unchecked columns and internal notes or ToDos are left out of the export. Settings can be saved, so monthly submissions come out in the exact same format every time.

The output is a standard .xlsx file where bars are drawn with cell fills. Anyone can open, review, and print it in Excel. When dates change, adjust them in Ploto and export again.

Bring In Your Existing Excel Schedule or WBS as Is

If you already have a schedule or WBS started in Excel, choose “Import from Excel” on the startup screen and pick your .xlsx file.

Ploto reads columns such as task name, start date, end date or duration, assignee, and progress. If the sheet has WBS numbers like 1.1 and 1.2, the hierarchy is restored as well. You can verify the columns in a preview and reassign any column that wasn’t detected automatically on the spot. Bars are redrawn from the date columns, so you don’t have to worry about manually colored cells.

When Ploto Is Not a Good Fit

  • Using outside Windows: There are no Mac or smartphone apps.
  • Editing simultaneously with multiple people: Data is saved to files on your PC. Cloud-based tools are better suited for real-time collaborative editing.
  • Cannot install apps on a company PC: In this case, you will need to keep working directly in Excel.
