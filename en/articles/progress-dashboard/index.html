A Living Strategy Board for Visualizing Progress | Ploto's Gantt-Linked Whiteboard

Wish you could lay out key tasks freely and see schedule delays or team workloads at a glance? Ploto's Whiteboard for Windows links directly with your Gantt chart to keep progress up to date.

Published: November 7, 2026

Visualizing Progress: Turning “I Wish I Could See It Like This” into Reality

Gantt charts are indispensable for managing an overall schedule, but as the number of rows grows, a table alone can make it hard to grasp the bigger picture.

“I want to pull out just the key tasks we need to focus on right now and arrange them freely on a single board.” “I want to sketch out workflows with shapes and arrows while seeing how far ahead or behind schedule we are on the same screen.”

The Whiteboard in Ploto, a project management app for Windows, turns that wish into reality. The cards and summary widgets you place on the board are connected to the exact same tasks in your Gantt chart. Update progress or dates in the schedule and the board updates automatically. There is no need to build a separate progress tracker or copy numbers over by hand just to visualize where things stand.

Creating Gantt charts and importing or exporting Excel files are free, while creating and editing whiteboards is a Pro (one-time purchase) feature. In the free version, you can view existing boards (display, pan, zoom, and live widget figures) and try out the Whiteboard hands-on in the built-in tutorial.

A Strategy Board with Living Tasks, Not Static Sticky Notes

Cards placed on a typical online whiteboard are static sticky notes that keep whatever text was written at the time. Ploto’s Whiteboard is a strategy board where you place your Gantt chart tasks themselves as cards anywhere you like. Each placed card continuously shows current progress, due dates, and assignees.

  1. Pick and place from the Task palette
    Open the Task palette on the left of the board and drag the tasks you want onto the canvas. Using “Place whole project” lays them out together while preserving their hierarchy.
  2. Parent tasks become containers
    Placing a parent task displays it as a container that groups its child tasks. Collapse it to shrink it to a single card with badges showing the number of children and their progress. Close containers to survey the whole board, or open them to examine the details.
  3. Draw in your thinking with shapes and connectors
    Place rectangles, ellipses, diamonds, lines, arrows, sticky notes, text, and formatted text freely. Because cards and shapes can be linked with connectors (lines) and labeled, you can express stage-to-stage flows and handovers on a single board.

Five Widgets That Read Out Status Without Opening the Schedule

Drop widgets onto the board and you can read “how many points behind schedule we are today” or “who has the heaviest workload” on the spot without switching back to the Gantt chart. Five widgets are available:

  • Schedule Progress: Shows actual progress alongside the figure your schedule calls for today, and states how many points you are ahead or behind. It also gathers the completed count, tasks in progress, overdue or due-soon counts, and remaining days of work.
  • Member Workload: Shows each member’s remaining work along with tasks in progress and waiting. Unassigned tasks are shown too.
  • Mini Gantt: Displays task schedules as a small Gantt chart. You can filter by assignee, tag, and period.
  • Kanban: Displays the columns and cards of an existing Kanban board.
  • Priority Matrix: Shows tasks placed on the Priority Matrix at their urgency and importance positions.

By default, a widget summarizes the whole board. If you want to focus on a specific stage or team, draw a line from a task card to the widget—only that task and those beneath it are counted. Connect widgets to different parent tasks and you can compare multiple groups side by side on a single board.

Paste Straight into Meeting Slides, or Share the File as Is

When you want to include the board in meeting materials or reports, select an area and export it as a PNG image. You can also make the background transparent so it drops cleanly onto slides or documents.

Anyone who receives your project file (.ploto) can also open and view the board in the free version of Ploto. Editing—including expanding and collapsing containers—is disabled, but display, panning, zooming, and live widget figures all work, so recipients can check the dashboard even without Pro.

If you already have a schedule in Excel, you can start by loading your .xlsx file from “Import from Excel” on the startup screen (free).

When Ploto Is Not a Good Fit

  • Using outside Windows: Ploto is a Windows-only app. There are no Mac or smartphone versions.
  • Editing simultaneously with multiple people: Data is saved to a file (.ploto) on your PC. It is not suited for real-time collaborative editing.
  • Drawing freeform diagrams unrelated to a schedule: If you are looking for a general-purpose online whiteboard disconnected from task management, a dedicated diagramming tool is a better fit.

Your Schedule Numbers Become a Board You Can Take In at a Glance

Lay out your timeline in the Gantt chart, and survey your key tasks and overall progress at a glance on the Whiteboard. Move a bar in the schedule and the cards and widget numbers on the board follow automatically.
