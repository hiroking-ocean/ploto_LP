How to Use the Whiteboard

The Whiteboard is a strategy board where you place tasks as cards anywhere you like. You can draw in relationships with shapes and sticky notes, or drop in summary widgets to turn it into a dashboard. What sets it apart from a general-purpose online whiteboard is that the cards are not static sticky notes: they continuously show current progress, due dates, and assignees. Update progress in the Gantt chart and the board follows automatically.

1. Creating a Board and Placing Tasks

Create a board first, then place only the tasks you actually need on the canvas.

Adding a Whiteboard

Click the 'Projects & Views' button on the left sidebar, then press the '+' button next to 'Whiteboard'.
*Creating and editing whiteboards are Pro features. The free version opens existing boards as view-only. You can also try it in the tutorial with the free version.

Placing Tasks from the Palette

Press the top button of the toolbar on the left of the board to open the 'Task palette', which lists your project's tasks in their hierarchy. Drag from there onto the canvas to place a card. 'Place whole project' lays them out together following the hierarchy. The palette lets you search by task name or narrow the list with 'Show unplaced only', so you can find what you need even with many tasks.
Switching to the tab never scatters every task onto the board automatically — you choose what goes on it.

Parent Tasks Become Containers

Placing a parent task displays it as a container holding its child tasks. Press the arrow to the left of the container's heading to expand or collapse it; collapsed, it shrinks to a single card with badges showing the number of children and their progress. Close containers to survey the whole board, open them to work on the details.
A container always reflects the task's entire subtree, so children you add later in the Gantt chart are filled into it automatically. Because of this, a child task cannot be removed from the board on its own — only the top task of a placed group can be removed.

2. Drawing In Your Thinking

Add the relationships and context that tasks alone cannot express.

Shapes, Lines, Sticky Notes, and Text

From the toolbar you can place rectangles, ellipses, diamonds, triangles, parallelograms, lines, arrows, elbow arrows, block arrows, sticky notes, text, and formatted text (with headings, lists and tables). Selecting one brings up a toolbar beside it where you can set fill color and opacity, border color, text color, alignment, bold, and italic; fonts can be chosen from default, gothic, mincho, textbook, handwriting, and monospace. Lines also offer arrowheads, line styles, and widths. Stacking order is adjusted with 'Bring to front', 'Send to back', and so on. Select several shapes and group them to make selection, movement, and deletion act as one.
Sticky notes and text scale their font size when resized. Labels on rectangles and ellipses do not, since resizing those is for creating room inside the box. You can also have the text size fit the shape automatically.

Connecting with Connectors

Drag one of the dots on the four sides of a card or shape to link it with a line (connector). While connecting, markers appear on all four sides of the target, and the line lands on whichever side is closest to where you drop — so it connects where you aimed. Connectors support toggling the arrowhead and adding a label, letting you draw flows such as 'once this is done, next comes this' or handovers between people.
Connect a sticky note to a task card and it shows that task's memo, which you can edit from the note (a confirmation appears first, because the note's existing text is removed).

Selecting, Moving, and Scaling

Pan and zoom the canvas freely. Dragging with the right button makes a rubber-band selection; selected items can be moved or deleted together, and dragging a corner of the bounding box scales everything selected proportionally.
Task cards and collapsed containers can be scaled between 25% and 200% by dragging a corner, with text scaling too. Dragging a corner of an expanded container changes the scale of its contents, and nesting multiplies the parent's scale down through it. Making important groups larger and reference material smaller gives the whole board a sense of emphasis. If you misstep, you can undo within the board.

3. Turning the Board into a Dashboard

Drop summary panels onto the board and it becomes a progress dashboard you can read at a glance.

Five Widgets

Choose from previews in the widget menu on the toolbar (the pie chart icon). Drag one onto the board, or click it and then click where you want it.

  • Schedule Progress:Shows the actual progress of the target tasks alongside the figure your schedule calls for today, and states how many points you are ahead or behind. It also gathers the completed count, tasks in progress, how many are overdue or due soon, and the remaining days of work.
  • Member Workload:Shows each member's remaining work along with tasks in progress and waiting. Unassigned tasks are shown too, making uneven workloads and assignment gaps visible.
  • Mini Gantt:Shows task schedules as a small Gantt chart. You can filter by assignee, tag, and period.
  • Kanban:Choose an existing Kanban board to show its columns and cards. Click a card to open its details.
  • Priority Matrix:Shows tasks placed on the priority matrix at their urgency and importance positions. Click a task to open its details.

Narrowing What a Widget Counts

By default a widget summarizes the whole board. To look at one area, draw a line from the dot on a task card to the widget: only the connected tasks and those beneath them are included, and removing the line releases them. Connecting a parent task alone reports on that entire group.
Line up widgets per team or per stage and a single board shows several perspectives at once.

Exporting as a PNG Image

Press 'Export PNG' on the toolbar and drag to set the area to export. You can also choose 'Whole board', or 'Selection' when nodes are selected. Check the preview, then copy the image or save it under a name. The background can be made transparent. Handy for pasting into meeting material or reports.

Sharing and Viewing Boards

Anyone you send the project file to can open the board and see its contents. In the free version boards are view-only: display, panning, zooming, and live widget figures still work, but editing, including expanding and collapsing containers, does not. If you are sharing a board as a dashboard, the recipient can read it without Pro.
